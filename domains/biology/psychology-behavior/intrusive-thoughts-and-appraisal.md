# Intrusive thoughts and appraisal

Unwanted intrusions "occur in the general population at a rate of at least 90%" (Williams et al. 2022, citing Rachman and de Silva 1978). The difference between a passing oddity and an obsession is interpretation: the non-clinical reading is "what an odd thought", the clinical one is "what if this is who I really am". Two named distortions carry it — moral thought-action fusion, equating thinking an act with doing it, and likelihood TAF, believing the thought raises the odds of the event.

The content is not random. The more firmly a value is held, the more likely a thought violating it is appraised as negative and personally relevant (Williams et al. 2022). An intrusion aims at what the person most cares about, which is exactly why it feels like evidence and exactly why it is not. The clinical formulation for aggressive obsessions is that the problem "reflects the fear of being dangerous", not the desire.

Compulsions in this dimension are mostly covert — mental review, somatic checking, neutralizing, distraction, thought suppression — plus overt reassurance-seeking and concealment. Concealment is itself a maintaining factor, and "dropping concealment" is a behavioural experiment: disclose to a close, non-critical person to test whether it changes how they see you. It is run only when success is likely (Williams et al. 2022). Compare [[verification-as-compulsion]]: the ritual is the response, not the thought.

## Tradeoffs

Only one RCT targets this subtype specifically. It tested Rachman's model and returned d = 2.34, durable at one year (Williams et al. 2022). A very large effect resting on a single trial — promising, not settled.

Misdiagnosis is a live hazard: among 360 APA-member providers, 38.9% misdiagnosed OCD vignettes, rising to 31.5% for aggressive obsessions and 77% for sexual-orientation obsessions, against 15.8% for contamination (Williams et al. 2022, citing Glazier et al. 2013). Clinician selection is therefore part of the intervention, not a preliminary to it.
