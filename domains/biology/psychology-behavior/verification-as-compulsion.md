# Verification as compulsion

Metacognitive therapy names its target the Cognitive-Attentional Syndrome: "dysfunctional coping strategies that a person employs as an attempt to manage distressful thoughts and feelings… worry, rumination, threat monitoring, thought control strategies, avoidance, and reassurance seeking" (Normann and Morina 2018). All six are responses to a thought, not thoughts. The clinical target is therefore the response.

This yields a discriminator that does not require introspection: structure that runs unattended is a habit; structure that must be checked is a ritual. Order itself is not the pathology — the verification loop attached to it is. The corresponding technique is detached mindfulness, in which "the patient is instructed to become aware of internal trigger thoughts and detach from them by taking a step back and disengaging any further coping or perseverative processing" (Normann and Morina 2018).

The naive alternative — suppress the thought — is on weaker ground than its popularity suggests. A 2024 review reports that "recent studies challenge the prevailing dogma that suppression leads to increased intrusion into awareness", faults the ambiguous instructions of the original white-bear paradigm, and argues suppression deserves reconsideration as a therapeutic tool (Mamat et al. 2024). So "don't suppress" should be justified by what works, not by an assumed rebound.

## Tradeoffs

MCT returned g = 2.06, 95% CI [1.52–2.60] against waitlist and g = 0.69 [0.36–1.03] against CBT at post-treatment, across 25 studies and 780 patients, 15 of them RCTs (Normann and Morina 2018). The same authors report "risk of bias was unclear or high in almost one third of the cases" and call for larger trials. Direction well supported; magnitude likely inflated.

The discriminator has a blind spot: a written system can be genuinely load-bearing and simultaneously serve an anxiolytic function. Removing it is then exposure, not simplification, and costs the scaffolding too. No source settles which case applies for a given person.
