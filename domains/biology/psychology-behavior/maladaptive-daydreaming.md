# Maladaptive daydreaming

Maladaptive daydreaming (MD) describes vivid, immersive, often plot-driven fantasy that consumes hours and displaces intended activity. It is distinguished from ordinary mind-wandering by absorption, deliberateness and duration.

The Maladaptive Daydreaming Scale runs 0–100, and "scores over 40 suggest suspected clinical-level MD" (Soffer-Dudek and Somer 2022). Across four samples totalling 1,023 respondents, the authors estimate point prevalence at 2.5% in the general Israeli-Jewish population, rising to 8.5% among 18–30 year-olds. Of those exceeding the cutoff who were then clinically interviewed, 60% met MD criteria.

The overlap with obsessive presentations is the most decision-relevant number: "in a sample of 510 MDers, over half of them exceeded the clinical cutoff for suspected OCD" (Soffer-Dudek and Somer 2022). Treating MD as an attention problem therefore misreads it; the shared mechanism is escape from an aversive internal state, which places it with avoidance behaviours and points the lever at the trigger rather than at concentration. ## Tradeoffs

The construct is young and the evidence thin. Only two of the four samples included clinical interviews, and the authors note results come from "modestly sized, mostly Jew or all Jew, Israeli samples" that "do not necessarily generalize" (Soffer-Dudek and Somer 2022). MD is not in DSM-5 or ICD-11.

The asymmetry matters in practice: the scale is validated, the syndrome is plausible, and no controlled treatment evidence exists at all. It is usable as a description and as a cue to look for what is being avoided; it is not usable as a diagnosis or as a treatment plan.
