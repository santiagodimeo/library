# Catastrophic misinterpretation

Arousal sensations — racing heart, breathlessness, dizziness, derealization — get read as heart attack, suffocation, insanity or imminent death, and the reading amplifies the sensation. Interoceptive exposure attacks the loop directly by inducing the sensations on purpose: hyperventilation and breath-holding for pseudoneurological symptoms, straw breathing for breathlessness, spinning for dizziness and dissociation, running in place for palpitations (Farris et al. 2025).

Across 132 studies, most exercises produced mild-to-moderate intensity (4–6 on 0–10) and panic occurred in roughly 20% of exercises. The safety data matter because the fear of harm is itself the target: dropout attributable to the exposure 1%, vomiting 0.1%, fainting 0.03%, seizure 0.03%, and "no serious adverse events were reported in any study" (Farris et al. 2025). The authors note that classical contraindications rested "largely on (untested) assumptions about safety risk". Strongest effects come from interoceptive exposure combined with cognitive restructuring, d = 0.88.

Health anxiety is the same engine with a longer time constant, maintained by body checking and reassurance-seeking; change in those two behaviours tracks clinical improvement. CBT returned g = 0.95 at post-treatment and g = 0.34 at follow-up across 15 comparisons from 13 RCTs, n = 1,081, with a dose-response relationship for number of sessions (Olatunji et al. 2014).

## Tradeoffs

The component network meta-analysis of 72 trials and 4,064 participants gives the counterintuitive result: muscle relaxation is associated with *worse* remission (iOR 0.59, 95% CrI 0.40–0.90) and breathing retraining does not improve efficacy (iOR 0.84, CrI 0.54–1.26) though it reduces dropout. Interoceptive exposure was the best component but its interval crosses one (iOR 1.49, CrI 0.94–2.36). Best versus worst package differed by OR 7.69 (CrI 1.75–33.33) on remission (Pompoli et al. 2018).

The practical implication is that relaxation and breath control taught as in-the-moment coping can function as safety behaviours — they relieve the episode and block the learning that the episode was survivable anyway. Direction is well supported; the per-component magnitudes are credible intervals from a network model, not head-to-head trials.
