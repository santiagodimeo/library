# Cognitive offloading

The best-studied natural experiment in offloading a cognitive skill is satellite navigation, not AI. In 50 adults, greater lifetime GPS use was associated with worse hippocampal-dependent spatial memory across three measures (r = −0.22 [−0.41, −0.01], r = −0.22 [−0.41, −0.02], r = −0.20 [−0.38, −0.02]), and the deficit appeared when navigating *without* GPS (Dahmani and Bohbot 2020). A 13-person follow-up at a mean 3.23 years argues against reverse causation: lifetime GPS experience did not correlate with self-reported sense of direction, so people were not reaching for GPS because they already felt lost.

Note what the effect is and is not. It is loss of the delegated capability, surfacing when the tool is withdrawn — not a general decline. Bainbridge's irony of automation predicts exactly this: mechanising the routine case removes the routine practice that builds judgement.

For generative AI the evidence is currently self-report. Across 319 knowledge workers contributing 936 first-hand examples, higher confidence in GenAI predicted less critical thinking while higher task-specific self-confidence predicted more; qualitatively, GenAI "shifts the nature of critical thinking toward information verification, response integration, and task stewardship" (Lee et al. 2025). The work relocates rather than disappears, which makes failing to notice the relocation the actual risk.

The countervailing effect is well replicated: producing an answer beats reading one. Retrieval practice runs about g = 0.50, holds at g = 0.50 in applied classrooms across 93 studies, and transfers to new questions at d = 0.40 (Green et al. 2025, reporting Rowland 2014, Yang et al. 2021, Pan and Rickard 2018). The operable rule follows — a tool may perform any step except generation of the answer being learned, and must come after the attempt, not before. That is inference from these sources; none tests the rule directly.

## Tradeoffs

The GPS longitudinal sample is 13 people and the authors concede it "was small", the follow-up "was unplanned", and "spurious correlations are possible". The AI evidence is correlational and self-reported. Nothing here licenses a claim that AI use lowers general intelligence.

Enforcement is the weak point. A generation-first rule is a rule the user evaluates while wanting to break it; see [[deterministic-mediation-of-agents]] for why that class of constraint decays.
